The challenge

The idea in one picture

The model knows the real size of the wall. Four corners connect the photo to it.

Say: This is the core idea. I place four dots on the wall corners in the photo. The model tells me the real width and height of that wall. From the four pairs I compute a homography and build a straight image where one pixel is one centimetre. Now windows are rectangles at their real size. (1 minute)

Decisions and reasons

A person places the corners

Decided

4 dots, dragged by a person, with a loupe

Why

Works on all three photos. A person sees the cars and the roof line. A wrong dot is easy to see and fix.

Rejected

Automatic corner detection. It fails where corners are hidden.

The loupe while I drag the top left corner

Say: Every number after this depends on the four corners, so I made placing them precise. The loupe came from using the tool myself: my hand covered the corner. (1 minute)
Decisions and reasons

My own homography, in lod2-core

Decided

8 equations from 4 point pairs, solved in my own code

Why

Small maths, no new dependency, tested within 1 pixel, and I can explain every line

Rejected

OpenCV: a big dependency for one small solve

homographyFromPairs(from, to) applyHomography(H, point) invertHomography(H)

Tested

Known points land within 1 pixel, and a round trip comes back

Say: The brief says libraries are fine, and I agree. Here the solve is small, so a library would add more than it saves. All maths lives in lod2-core, because core has tests and the viewer has none. (1 minute)
Decisions and reasons

Openings drawn by hand, on the straight wall

Decided

Rectangles drawn by a person, stored in wall metres

Why

Detectors fail behind cars, skips and trees. My boxes are the reference a detector is tested against.

Rejected

Automatic detection first. It is my next step, on the rectified image.

Say: I decided to do the rectification properly first, because every number depends on it. On the straight image windows are rectangles at real size, so detection becomes much easier there. That is the natural next step, and my boxes are the ground truth to test it. (1 minute 20 seconds)
Decisions and reasons

The key finding: measure above the ground

LoD2 has no basement, so the model wall goes below the street. On kY it went 10.94 m down: 26.22 m became 15.28 m.

Say: This was the most important thing I found. On kY the wall came out 26 metres high, with 5 metre floors. That is not a real building. The info panel said the building is up to 11 metres deep below ground. The model's wall goes down to the floor slab, but my bottom dots were at the street. So everything was stretched by 1.72. Now I cut the wall at the model's own ground contact line. Here you see BOo: it was stretched too, by 8 percent, so every window was taller than it really is. (1 minute 40 seconds)

Say: I measured every wall twice. The ground fix cannot change the ratio, because it scales windows and wall the same way, so the difference between my two runs is pure drawing error: one to two percentage points. Identical windows on one floor differ by up to 17 centimetres, so that is the precision of one box. kY's ratio is too low, because a car and a shadow hide the ground floor and I drew nothing there.
